Medical & Inclusion Policy

How Flag Frenzy Football Camp manages medical information, allergies, medication, illness and injury while supporting children with additional needs and disabilities.

Flag Frenzy Football Camp

Our Commitment

Safe, Inclusive Participation

Flag Frenzy Football Camp wants children to be able to take part safely, confidently and enjoyably.

We recognise that children may have medical conditions, allergies, disabilities, additional needs or other circumstances that affect how they participate in camp. Parents and guardians are encouraged to tell us about relevant needs as early as possible so that appropriate arrangements can be discussed before attendance.

Please tell us about relevant medical or additional needs before your child attends camp. Having accurate information helps us consider what support, adjustments or safety arrangements may be needed.

01

Purpose & Scope

This policy explains how Flag Frenzy Football Camp approaches medical information, allergies, medication, illness, injury, emergency care and inclusion.

It applies to children attending camp and to parents, guardians and members of the camp team involved in supporting children's health, safety and participation.

02

Information From Parents & Guardians

Parents and guardians are responsible for providing accurate, relevant and up-to-date information about their child.

This may include:

  • medical conditions and relevant medical history;
  • allergies and known triggers;
  • prescribed medication that may be required during camp;
  • disabilities, SEND or other additional needs;
  • recent illness or injury that may affect participation;
  • relevant professional or healthcare guidance; and
  • emergency contact information for a parent, guardian or another authorised adult who can be reached during camp.

Parents and guardians should let us know if relevant information changes before or during the camp period.

03

Allergies & Medical Conditions

Known allergies and medical conditions that could affect a child's safety or participation should be disclosed before attendance.

Relevant information will be made available to members of the camp team who need it in order to support the child safely.

Where a child has a significant allergy or medical condition, we may contact the parent or guardian before camp to discuss their needs, emergency arrangements and any medication or healthcare instructions.

We cannot guarantee that a camp environment will be completely free from a particular allergen. Parents should therefore provide full information about severe allergies and any emergency medication or action plan required.

04

Medication

If a child needs medication while attending camp, the parent or guardian should tell us in advance wherever possible.

Medication brought to camp should normally:

  • be clearly labelled for the child;
  • be supplied in appropriate original packaging where practicable;
  • include clear instructions about the required dose, timing and method of administration; and
  • be handed to the appropriate member of the camp team rather than left loose in a child's bag, unless an agreed arrangement allows the child to keep emergency medication with them.

Arrangements for administering or supervising medication will depend on the medication, the child's needs and what can reasonably and safely be provided within the camp setting.

Where medication is administered by the camp team, an appropriate record should be made.

Parents should discuss medication requirements with us before camp where possible, particularly for medication that may require specialist knowledge, storage or administration.

05

Emergency Medication

Children who require emergency medication, such as an inhaler or adrenaline auto-injector, must have the required medication available at camp.

The appropriate arrangements for access to emergency medication will be agreed according to the child's age, needs, the medication involved and any relevant healthcare instructions.

Parents and guardians are responsible for ensuring medication supplied for their child is appropriate for use and has not expired.

06

Inclusion, SEND & Disabilities

Flag Frenzy Football Camp welcomes enquiries and registrations for children with SEND, disabilities and other additional needs.

We encourage parents and guardians to tell us about their child's needs before attendance so that we can understand what support may be required and consider reasonable adjustments.

Depending on the individual child, adjustments could include:

  • adapting an activity or coaching instruction;
  • allowing additional processing or transition time;
  • using visual or simplified instructions;
  • planning rest or quieter periods;
  • adapting how a child participates in a particular activity; or
  • considering appropriate supervision or support arrangements.

Adjustments are considered individually. What is reasonable and safe will depend on the child's needs, the activity, staffing, venue and other relevant circumstances.

07

Additional or One-to-One Support

Standard camp attendance does not automatically include dedicated one-to-one support.

If a child ordinarily requires substantial individual support to participate safely, parents and guardians should contact us before booking or attendance so that the child's needs can be discussed.

We will consider what arrangements may reasonably be possible. This may include discussing adaptations, different support arrangements or whether an appropriate support person could attend subject to safeguarding, suitability and venue requirements.

We will not knowingly promise a level of support that the camp is not reasonably able to provide safely.

08

Individual Medical or Support Planning

Where a child has a significant or ongoing medical condition or additional need, we may ask for further information before attendance.

This may include an existing healthcare or emergency action plan, relevant professional guidance, details of triggers and symptoms, medication instructions and emergency contact arrangements.

Where appropriate, relevant information may be summarised into a camp-specific plan so that those responsible for the child understand the agreed arrangements.

09

First Aid, Accidents & Emergencies

Appropriate first-aid arrangements will be considered as part of the planning for each camp.

If a child becomes ill or is injured during camp, the response will depend on the circumstances and may include:

  • providing appropriate immediate first aid;
  • stopping or adapting the child's participation;
  • contacting the parent, guardian or emergency contact;
  • asking for the child to be collected; and
  • contacting emergency services where urgent medical assistance is required.

Emergency situations: where urgent treatment is required, the priority will be obtaining appropriate medical assistance. We will attempt to contact the child's parent or guardian as soon as reasonably possible.

10

Illness & Infection

Children should not attend camp when they are acutely unwell, have an infectious illness that requires exclusion, or are not well enough to participate safely in normal camp activities.

If a child becomes unwell during camp, we may contact their parent, guardian or emergency contact and ask for the child to be collected.

Vomiting or diarrhoea: children should not attend until at least 48 hours have passed since their last episode of vomiting or diarrhoea.

For other infectious conditions, parents should follow appropriate NHS or public-health guidance and tell us about relevant circumstances where this may affect other children or the safe operation of camp.

11

Injury & Return to Activity

Parents should tell us about a recent injury or medical restriction that may affect their child's participation.

Where appropriate, activities may be adapted to reflect the child's current needs and relevant medical advice.

A child should not be encouraged to participate through an injury where doing so could place them at additional risk.

Head Injury

Suspected Concussion

A suspected concussion or significant head injury will be treated seriously.

If in doubt, sit them out.

A child with a suspected concussion will be removed from sporting activity and will not return to play during that session.

The child's parent or guardian will be informed and appropriate medical advice should be sought. Emergency medical assistance will be requested where symptoms or circumstances indicate that this is necessary.

Return to sport following a suspected or confirmed concussion should follow appropriate medical advice and recognised graduated return-to-activity guidance.

12

Medical, Accident & Incident Records

Appropriate records may be kept where medical information is supplied or where first aid, medication, an accident or a significant health-related incident occurs during camp.

Records may include relevant information about what happened, any treatment or action taken, medication administered, communication with parents and any further action required.

Significant incidents may also be considered under our Safeguarding or Health & Safety procedures where appropriate.

13

Medical Information & Privacy

Medical and additional-needs information can be sensitive personal information and will be handled accordingly.

Information will be accessed or shared within the camp team where there is a legitimate need to know it in order to support the child's health, safety or participation.

Information may also be shared with emergency services, healthcare professionals, safeguarding services or other appropriate bodies where this is necessary for the child's care, safety or where otherwise permitted or required by law.

For further information about how personal information is handled, see our Privacy Policy .

14

Policy Review

This policy will be reviewed at least annually and sooner where necessary, including following a significant incident, changes to camp arrangements or relevant changes to guidance.
